title: "Payments"
description: "Accept payments via PIX, credit and debit cards, and boleto using YuvexPay."
---

A payment is a charge against a payer for a fixed amount in BRL. YuvexPay
exposes a single `POST /v1/payments` endpoint that creates payments across
three methods (`PIX`, `CARD`, `BOLETO`) and two presentation modes (`headless`
and `hosted`).

## Methods at a glance

| Method | Headless | Hosted |
|--------|----------|--------|
| `PIX`     | Returns the BR-Code (copy-paste) and a QR code image. You render them in your own UI. | Returns a `checkoutUrl` to the YuvexPay-hosted checkout. |
| `CARD`    | Not available. Use the hosted checkout. | The payer enters card details on YuvexPay's hosted page. See [Card payments](#card-payments). |
| `BOLETO`  | Returns a boleto barcode and a downloadable PDF. | Available through the hosted checkout. |

Pick `headless` when you want to render the payment yourself; pick `hosted`
when you want YuvexPay to render the checkout, including method selection.

## Amounts and currency

- All amounts are in **BRL** as decimal numbers (e.g. `49.90`), not cents.
- The minimum chargeable amount is `R$ 0.01`. The maximum is `R$ 1,000,000.00`.
- Card payments go through the hosted checkout only. They are charged to a credit card in a single installment (à vista) or to a debit card, and need an `amount` of at least `R$ 5.00`.

## Identifiers

Every payment has two identifiers:

- `id` — an internal UUID. Use it as the path parameter for `GET /v1/payments/{paymentId}` and `POST /v1/payments/{paymentId}/refund`.
- `txId` — a stable, prefixed transaction id (`PAY` + 32 hex characters) that identifies the payment across logs, reports and the dashboard.

You can also pass `externalId` on creation — typically your own order id —
which is stored on the payment for cross-referencing. `externalId` is unique
per company and environment.

## Lifecycle

```
NEW
  └── PROCESSING ──► CONFIRMED ──► PAID ──► PARTIAL_REFUND ──► REFUNDED
                                       │
                                       ├── CHARGEBACK
                                       └── MED_FROZEN
  └── PENDING_METHOD_SELECTION ──► (one of the above, after the payer picks a method)
                               └──► EXPIRED (no method picked before expiresAt)
  └── EXPIRED
  └── CANCELLED
```

| Status | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| `NEW` | Created and waiting for the payer. |
| `PENDING_METHOD_SELECTION` | A hosted multi-method checkout is created and the payer hasn't picked a method yet. |
| `PROCESSING` | The card or PIX network is processing the charge, or the outcome of a boleto charge is not known yet after a `502` (see [Boleto errors](#boleto-errors)). |
| `CONFIRMED` | The payment was authorized but funds are not yet settled (typical for cards/boleto). |
| `PAID` | Funds were received and credited to your balance. |
| `EXPIRED` | The payment window passed without payment. |
| `CANCELLED` | The payment was cancelled before being paid. |
| `PARTIAL_REFUND` | A partial refund has been issued. No further refund is possible. |
| `REFUNDED` | A full refund has been issued. |
| `CHARGEBACK` | A chargeback was filed and processed. If the card dispute is won, the payment returns to the status it had before the chargeback. |
| `MED_FROZEN` | A PIX MED (special refund) is open; funds are temporarily held. |

Listen for the corresponding [webhook events](/guides/webhooks) to react to
status changes asynchronously.

## Headless PIX

Headless PIX is the simplest flow. Create the payment, render the QR code or
copy-paste string, and listen for `PAYMENT_PAID`:

```bash
curl -X POST https://api.yuvexpay.com/v1/payments \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $YUVEX_API_KEY" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -H "X-Idempotency-Key: order-1234" \
  -d '{
    "amount": 49.90,
    "methods": ["PIX"],
    "currency": "BRL",
    "mode": "headless",
    "description": "Order #1234",
    "externalId": "order-1234",
    "expiresInMinutes": 30,
    "customer": {
      "name": "Pedro Álvares Cabral",
      "email": "user@example.com"
    }
  }'
```

The response includes `methodData.pixCopyPaste` (the BR-Code string) and
`methodData.qrCodeBase64` (a data URI). Render either to the payer.

A PIX payment whose create returns `502` or `503 PROVIDER_TEMPORARILY_UNAVAILABLE`
is recorded as `CANCELLED`: the charge was not created, so retry with a new
`externalId`.

## Boleto errors

| Response | Meaning |
|----------|---------|
| `400 PROVIDER_REJECTED` | The payment was refused. The payment is recorded as `CANCELLED`. The message is generic and does not say why. |
| `503 PROVIDER_TEMPORARILY_UNAVAILABLE` | The charge was not created. The payment is recorded as `CANCELLED`. Retry later. |
| `502 PROVIDER_TEMPORARILY_UNAVAILABLE` | The outcome is not known yet. The payment stays `PROCESSING` until it is resolved, which can take 15 minutes or more, and then either continues normally or ends `CANCELLED`. |

`methodData` is `null` while a boleto payment is `PROCESSING` after a `502`,
and on a payment recorded `CANCELLED` before a charge was created.

A create request that reuses an `externalId` returns the existing payment
instead of creating another one. Send `externalId` on every boleto payment.
After a `400` or a `503`, use a new `externalId` to try again.

The `502` is stored for its `X-Idempotency-Key`: repeating the request with the
same `X-Idempotency-Key` returns the same `502` for 24 hours and never submits
a second charge. To recover the payment after a `502`:

1. Repeat the request once with the same `externalId` and a new
   `X-Idempotency-Key`. The response is the existing payment with its `id`, and
   no second charge is submitted. Its `methodData` is `null` while it is
   `PROCESSING`.
2. Poll `GET /v1/payments/{paymentId}` until the status leaves `PROCESSING`. Do
   not poll by repeating the create: a repeated create with the same key
   replays the same response for 24 hours.
3. If the payment ends `CANCELLED`, create a new payment with a new
   `externalId`.

Without `externalId`, a request with a new `X-Idempotency-Key` creates a new
payment, and the payer can end up with two boletos for the same order.

## Hosted checkout

Set `mode: "hosted"` to receive a `checkoutUrl`. Redirect the payer there;
YuvexPay handles method selection, card capture and PIX rendering.

```json
{
  "amount": 49.90,
  "methods": ["PIX", "CARD"],
  "currency": "BRL",
  "mode": "hosted",
  "description": "Order #1234",
  "returnUrl": "https://store.example.com/checkout/return",
  "completionUrl": "https://store.example.com/checkout/done",
  "customer": {
    "name": "Pedro Álvares Cabral",
    "email": "user@example.com",
    "document": "12345678900"
  }
}
```

- `returnUrl` is where YuvexPay sends the payer if they abandon the checkout.
- `completionUrl` is where YuvexPay redirects after a successful payment.

Hosted payments start in `PENDING_METHOD_SELECTION` until the payer picks a
method, then transition through the same lifecycle as a headless payment. If
the payer doesn't pick a method before `expiresAt`, the payment moves to
`EXPIRED` shortly after and you receive `PAYMENT_EXPIRED`.

### Card payments

The hosted checkout is the only way to accept a card. `POST /v1/payments`
rejects card data, and `CARD` in headless mode, with
`400 CARD_PAYMENTS_NOT_AVAILABLE_VIA_API` (see
[Payment errors](/guides/errors#payment-errors)), so your servers never handle
or store card data.

To charge a card:

1. Create the payment with `mode: "hosted"` and `CARD` in `methods`: `["CARD"]`,
   or `["PIX", "CARD"]` to let the payer choose. The `amount` must be at least
   `R$ 5.00`.
2. Redirect the payer to the returned `checkoutUrl`. The checkout collects the
   card and runs 3D Secure itself. It accepts credit cards, charged in a
   single installment, and debit cards.
3. Read the result from the `PAYMENT_CONFIRMED`, `PAYMENT_PAID` and
   `PAYMENT_CANCELLED` webhooks, or poll `GET /v1/payments/{paymentId}`.

`returnUrl` and `completionUrl` work as for any hosted payment. A hosted card
payment that fails the 3D Secure challenge, or is refused after it, ends
`CANCELLED` and you receive `PAYMENT_CANCELLED` with
`reason: "payment_failed"`. The payer cannot pick another method for that
payment: create a new one.

Payment links created in the dashboard open the same hosted checkout.

Card payments must be enabled for your account; until they are, a request with
`CARD` in `methods` returns `403 PAYMENT_METHOD_NOT_ENABLED`. Card acceptance
also requires the account owner's e-mail to be confirmed, and YuvexPay's risk
controls can suspend it temporarily. On an account with card payments enabled,
while card acceptance is unavailable, other methods keep working, and:

- The hosted checkout does not offer `CARD`, even on payments and payment links
  created while it was available.
- Creating a payment with `CARD` and other methods succeeds without `CARD`.
- Creating a payment whose only method is `CARD` returns
  `403 PAYMENT_METHOD_NOT_ENABLED` (see
  [Payment errors](/guides/errors#payment-errors)).

Card payments already created with card data through `POST /v1/payments` keep
working: `GET` returns their masked card `methodData`, and webhooks, refunds and
chargebacks are unchanged.

On a card payment, `GET /v1/payments/{paymentId}` returns
`methodData.cardFundingType`: `credit` when the card was charged as a credit
card, `debit` when it was charged as a debit card, and `null` when the funding
type is not known, including older payments. Webhook bodies do not carry it.

## Fees and net amount

Each payment response includes:

- `amount` — the gross amount charged to the payer.
- `feeAmount` — the YuvexPay processing fee.
- `netAmount` — the amount credited to your balance once the payment settles,
  before any [splits](#splits).

Fees are computed at creation time using the merchant's configured rate.
`passFeeToPayer: true` shifts the fee onto the payer (the payer is charged
`amount + feeAmount`); when `false`, the fee is deducted from `amount`.

A refund you issue returns the fee in proportion to the refunded amount. A PIX
MED that returns funds and a chargeback do not: your balance is debited the
full amount returned to the payer, and YuvexPay keeps `feeAmount`.

## Splits

A payment can hand part of itself to other YuvexPay companies. Send `splits` on
`POST /v1/payments`; each entry names the company that receives a share and how
much of the payment it gets.

```json
{
  "amount": 49.90,
  "methods": ["PIX"],
  "mode": "headless",
  "splits": [
    {
      "recipientCompanyId": "b7f1c3d2-4e5a-4b6c-8d9e-0a1b2c3d4e5f",
      "type": "PERCENTAGE",
      "value": 10
    },
    {
      "recipientCompanyId": "c9a2d4e6-8b1f-4c3a-9d7e-2f5a8b0c1d3e",
      "type": "FIXED",
      "value": 5.00
    }
  ]
}
```

| Field | Value | Meaning |
|-------|-------|---------|
| `recipientCompanyId` | uuid | The YuvexPay company that receives the share. Must be a company other than your own, and unique within the array. |
| `type` | `PERCENTAGE` or `FIXED` | How `value` is read. Both forms can coexist in the same array. |
| `value` | `0.01`–`100` for `PERCENTAGE`; `0.01` or more for `FIXED` | A percentage of `amount`, or a BRL amount. At most two decimal places either way. |

`splits` is optional. The maximum number of entries is the recipient limit
configured for your account — three by default, and support can raise it.
Splits are set at creation only: there is no endpoint to add, edit or remove one
afterwards. The create response and the payment detail response echo them back
as `splits`, each entry carrying an `id` alongside the three fields you sent,
and `[]` when the payment has none.

### How the money divides

The YuvexPay fee is always the originating company's and is never apportioned.
Shares are computed from `amount` — the gross — and the fee comes out of your
share alone. When `passFeeToPayer` is true, nothing is deducted from `amount` at
all.

`netAmount` stays pre-split: it is still `amount - feeAmount`, or `amount` when
the fee is passed to the payer. You keep `netAmount` minus the sum of the
splits, and the recipients are credited when the payment settles.

For `amount: 49.90` against a `2.99` fee, split 10% to one company and a fixed
`R$ 5.00` to another:

| Field | Value | Meaning |
|-------|-------|---------|
| `amount` | `49.90` | The gross. Percentage shares are computed from it. |
| `feeAmount` | `2.99` | Yours. Never apportioned. |
| `netAmount` | `46.91` | `amount - feeAmount`, before splits. |
| First split (`PERCENTAGE`, `10`) | `4.99` | 10% of `49.90`. |
| Second split (`FIXED`, `5.00`) | `5.00` | Taken as sent. |
| Your share | `36.92` | `netAmount` minus both splits. |

The sum of the splits must be at most `netAmount`. A request that goes over
fails with `SPLIT_AMOUNT_EXCEEDS_NET` and the response names the overflow.
Nothing is clamped.

Percentage shares are rounded **down** to the cent, and every remaining cent
stays with the originating company. On `amount: 100.00` with a `2.99` fee and
one recipient at `33.33%`, the recipient is credited `33.33` and you keep
`97.01 - 33.33 = 63.68`.

### Split partners

You can only split to a company that has agreed to receive splits from you. The
pair is created in the dashboard, under **Configurações &gt; Parceiros de
divisão**: the originating company invites the recipient by its **company ID**
— the same ID the recipient sees under **Configurações &gt; Empresa** — and the
recipient accepts. The recipient never initiates.

Consent is per environment and per direction. A recipient that accepted in
production still has to accept in sandbox, and accepting your splits does not
let it split to you.

[`GET /v1/splits/partners`](/api-reference/splits/partners) lists the companies
your account may split with, so an integration knows which IDs are usable
without hardcoding them. Inviting, accepting, declining and revoking are
dashboard-only: accepting is a human act.

There is one exception. A platform approved by YuvexPay connects a seller on
the seller's first payment, with no invitation, by sending its own API key in
the `X-Split-Platform-Key` header of `POST /v1/payments`. That connection
exists in production only and carries a fee cap: a split to an approved
platform above the cap fails with `SPLIT_FEE_CAP_EXCEEDED`, and one to a
platform whose approval was withdrawn fails with `SPLIT_PLATFORM_SUSPENDED`.
See [Split platforms](/guides/split-platforms).

A relationship is in one of five states.

| Status | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `PENDING` | Invited, waiting for the recipient to answer. |
| `ACCEPTED` | Active. Only this state can be named in `splits[]`. |
| `DECLINED` | The recipient said no. |
| `REVOKED` | An active pair was ended, by either side. |
| `CANCELLED` | The originator withdrew the invite before it was answered. |

Invites do not expire; the originator can cancel one at any time. After
`DECLINED`, `REVOKED` or `CANCELLED` the originator can invite the same company
again — that starts a **new** relationship and the earlier one is kept as
history.

Creating a payment naming a recipient that has not accepted fails with
`SPLIT_RELATIONSHIP_PENDING`, one naming a recipient that declined the
invitation fails with `SPLIT_RELATIONSHIP_DECLINED`, and one naming a recipient
whose pair was revoked fails with `SPLIT_RELATIONSHIP_REVOKED`. Revoked is
reported ahead of declined, and declined ahead of pending.
`SPLIT_RELATIONSHIP_DECLINED` carries `details.recipients`, the company IDs that
refused; the pair becomes usable again only once you send a new invitation from
the dashboard and that company accepts it.

`SPLIT_RELATIONSHIP_PENDING` is still the single answer for every other
recipient that cannot receive a split yet. Declined and revoked are only
reachable for a company your own company invited first, so neither tells you
anything you did not already know, and a company you never invited reads the
same as a company ID that belongs to no company at all — so the error cannot be
used to discover which company IDs exist.

In production, `POST /v1/payments` never opens a split request. Naming a
recipient your company has no relationship with returns
`SPLIT_RELATIONSHIP_PENDING` and creates nothing: no request and no e-mail to
the recipient. Send the invitation from the dashboard. In sandbox the call
returns the same error and also opens the pending request, which the recipient
accepts in its dashboard.

A production request that a payment opened in the past cannot be accepted any
more: the recipient's dashboard answers `SPLIT_RELATIONSHIP_REINVITE_REQUIRED`.
Cancel it and send a new invitation from the dashboard.

<Note>
  Revocation is forward-only, and either side can revoke. Payments that were
  already created still settle, refund and freeze normally, and the recipient
  still receives those shares — a split is fixed at creation.
</Note>

Your company can hold a limited number of split requests awaiting an answer, 20
by default and counted per environment. Beyond that, a new invitation is
refused until some of the outstanding requests are answered. On
`POST /v1/payments` the limit is reachable in sandbox only, where naming a new
recipient opens a request: the call fails with
`SPLIT_RELATIONSHIP_REQUEST_LIMIT`. A production payment never opens a request,
so it never returns this code.

The number of *active* partners is capped too: three by default, the same
recipient limit that bounds `splits[]`. The dashboard enforces that ceiling
when an invite is sent or accepted. The API returns it in one case: a
[platform connection](/guides/split-platforms) that would take the seller past
the limit fails with `SPLIT_PARTNER_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`, and `details.limit`
carries the limit.

Both companies must be approved before a split payment can be created: KYC for
an individual, KYC and KYB for a business, on both sides. The bar is checked
when the invite is sent and again when the payment is created, not when it
settles. Your own company failing it returns
`403 SPLIT_ORIGINATOR_NOT_APPROVED`. A recipient that has not accepted returns
`SPLIT_RELATIONSHIP_PENDING` whatever the reason, or
`SPLIT_RELATIONSHIP_DECLINED` if it refused your invitation; one that had
accepted and has since stopped meeting the bar returns
`SPLIT_RECIPIENT_NOT_APPROVED`.

In sandbox the flow is identical but the KYC gate does not apply, so the whole
invite → accept → split cycle is testable with unverified sandbox companies.

### What a recipient sees

A split is not the recipient's payment, and recipients get no webhook and no
receipt for it.

- `GET /v1/payments/{paymentId}` returns `404` for a recipient: the payment
  belongs to the originating company.
- The payment receipt is originator-only.
- Recipients see the money in [`GET /v1/balance`](/api-reference/balance/get)
  and in [`GET /v1/documents/statements`](/api-reference/documents/statement),
  and in their dashboard.

<Warning>
  Don't build a recipient integration that waits for `PAYMENT_PAID`. No webhook
  is delivered to a recipient for a split. Read the balance or the statement
  instead.
</Warning>

### Refunds, MED and reserve

Refunds and PIX MED are proportional. A refund debits every party in proportion
to what it received, the originating company included, and partial refunds use
the same proportions. A MED freezes each party's proportional share.

A recipient that has already withdrawn its share goes negative. The shortfall is
never moved onto the originating company, and
`INSUFFICIENT_BALANCE_FOR_REFUND` is measured against the originating company's
share only.

A split credit is subject to the **recipient's own** rolling reserve policy, not
the originator's. When a refund or MED debit lands on a recipient whose share is
still in reserve, that payment's own reserve hold is released first and the
debit is written against it.

### Sandbox

A sandbox payment can only split to a company that is in sandbox mode, and a
production payment only to a production company. The other direction is always
rejected, never a silent no-op: `400 SPLIT_RELATIONSHIP_PENDING` for a recipient
that has not accepted in this environment, and `400 SPLIT_ENVIRONMENT_MISMATCH`
for one that had. Sandbox splits never touch a production balance, statement or
report.

The invite, accept and split cycle works in sandbox. The
[platform connection](/guides/split-platforms) does not: `X-Split-Platform-Key`
is ignored on a request made with a `ypk_test_` key, and the recipient accepts
in its dashboard as any other partner does.

## Pagination and filtering

`GET /v1/payments` supports the standard `page` (default `1`) and `limit`
(default `20`, max `100`) parameters along with these filters:

- `status` — filter by `PaymentStatus`.
- `method` — filter by `PIX`, `CARD` or `BOLETO`.
- `startDate` / `endDate` — ISO 8601 date or date-time bounds.
- `minAmount` / `maxAmount` — decimal BRL bounds.
- `environment` — `SANDBOX` or `PRODUCTION` (defaults to the API key's environment).

## Receipts

Once a payment is `PAID` or `REFUNDED`, you can fetch a printable receipt:

```
GET /v1/documents/payments/{paymentId}/receipt?format=html
```

See the [Documents reference](/api-reference/documents/payment-receipt) for the
full response shape.
